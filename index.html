Evaluation Prompt

We use an LLM-based evaluator with the following prompt: 

You are a math problem grader. Given a student's solution and the correct answer, determine if the solution contains the correct answer.

Student's Solution:
{solution}

Correct Answer:
{ground_truth}

Instructions:
1. Check if the student's solution arrives at the correct answer
2. The answer format may differ (e.g., "3.14" vs "π", "1/2" vs "0.5"), but the value should be equivalent
3. Respond with ONLY "CORRECT" or "INCORRECT"

Lexical vs. Semantic Consistency

We construct controlled examples to highlight the limitation of lexical similarity and the importance of semantic correctness. Semantically equivalent statements may have different surface forms, while lexically similar statements may still differ in factual correctness.

Word Order Variations (Semantically Equivalent)

  • The capital of Italy is Rome
  • Rome is the capital of Italy
  • Italy's capital is Rome
  • Rome is Italy's capital
  • Italy has Rome as its capital

Factual Variations (Lexically Similar but Incorrect)

  • The capital of Italy is Rome
  • The capital of Italy is Naples
  • The capital of Italy is Milan
  • The capital of Italy is Venice
  • The capital of Italy is Turin

This example illustrates that lexical similarity alone is insufficient for reliable hallucination detection, motivating our representation-level approach.
